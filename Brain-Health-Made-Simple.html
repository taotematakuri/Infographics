Brain Health Made Simple

Genes and age matter, but your daily habits matter too. The same things that help your heart and your weight, like moving, sleeping, and eating real food, also protect your brain.

Your weight loss work is brain work too.

A major 2024 report found that almost half of dementia cases are linked to risks people can change. Five of them are things our programs work on every week: extra weight, blood sugar, blood pressure, LDL cholesterol, and inactivity.1

Step 2

On a GLP-1? What we know about your brain.

You may have heard GLP-1s could prevent Alzheimer's. In 2025, two large trials found that semaglutide pills did not slow Alzheimer's in people who already had early disease.2 The proven brain benefits still come from lowering your known risks.

Foggy? Check the basics

Eating very little and not drinking enough can leave you tired and foggy, especially early on. Steady fluids and enough protein help.

Track the numbers that matter

Weight, blood pressure, A1c, and LDL. As they improve, your brain risk goes down with them.

Step 3

Keep your brain growing

Your brain can build new connections at any age. Scientists call it neuroplasticity.

Best proof

Exercise

In one trial, a year of walking grew the memory center of the brain by about 2% in older adults.3 Aim for 150 minutes a week.4

Learn something new

A language, a dance class, an instrument. New and hard is the point.

Read and play

Books, puzzles, card games, chess. Brain games mostly make you better at that game, so mix it up.

Change your routine

A new route to work, brushing with your other hand. Small bits of novelty add up.

Step 4

Let your brain recharge

Best proof

Sleep seven or more hours

Adults need at least seven hours.5 During deep sleep, the brain clears waste that builds up during the day (shown in animal studies).6

Best proof

Stay connected

Social isolation is one of the 14 dementia risks. Game nights, shared meals, church, a weekly walk with a friend.

Quiet your mind

Meditation or prayer can ease stress and may help attention. Apps like Insight Timer, Calm, and Headspace can get you started.

Get outside

Time in nature lowers stress and lifts mood. Walk, garden, ride, camp.

Step 5

Brain-friendly foods

These foods form the MIND diet. In a 2023 trial, people on the MIND diet and people on a regular healthy diet both improved their thinking scores while losing a little weight, by about the same amount.7 Eating well and losing weight seemed to matter more than any one food.

Leafy greensBerriesSalmon and fatty fishExtra virgin olive oilNutsBeansWhole grainsEggsBroccoliAvocadosTomatoesBeetsSeedsCoffee or green teaA little dark chocolateTurmeric and rosemary
Limit: fried food, pastries and sweets, processed meat, and too much alcohol.
Step 6

Brain supplements, checked

No supplement has been proven to prevent memory loss or dementia.8 Food first. If you want to try one, talk with your provider, since some interact with medicines.

Partly true
Omega-3 (fish oil)
Eating fish is linked to healthier brain aging. Fish oil pills haven't slowed memory decline in healthy adults in most trials.8 Two servings of fatty fish a week is a good goal.
Partly true
B vitamins
They help if you're low. B12 runs low more often with age, a vegan diet, and the diabetes medicine metformin. In people with memory trouble and high homocysteine, B vitamins slowed brain shrinkage in one trial.9 Ask about a B12 check.
Partly true
Bacopa monnieri
A small memory benefit showed up in some trials after 12 weeks.10 It can upset your stomach.
Not proven
Resveratrol
Studies in people are small and mixed. Berries and grapes give you some anyway.8
Fun facts

Your brain by the numbers

Weight

About 3 pounds

With a texture like firm gelatin.

Fat

About 60% of its dry weight

Most of your brain is water. Of what's left, much is fat, which is one reason healthy fats matter.

Speed

Up to 268 mph

The fastest nerve signals travel at about 120 meters per second.

Power

About 20 watts

Roughly a dim light bulb, running all day and night.11
