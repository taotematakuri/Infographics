Intermittent Fasting, Made Simple

Intermittent fasting is choosing when you eat. Your kitchen is open for part of the day and closed for the rest. That's it.

Pillar: Time Your Meals Right
!
The clock is a helper, not magic.

When two groups eat the same calories, people who fast lose about the same weight as people who don't.1,2 Fasting works when a shorter window makes it easier for you to eat a little less. Pick it because it fits your life.

Step 1

Pick your window

Each ring is one full day. Midnight is at the top. Green is when you eat. Start with the easiest one and stay there until it feels normal.

Kitchen open Kitchen closed (water, black coffee, tea)
What about 5:2 or 24-hour fasts? Some people fast two days a week or go a full day without food. These are harder, cause more side effects, and make it tough to get enough protein. If you take a GLP-1, skip them unless your prescriber says otherwise.
Step 2

On a GLP-1? Read this first.

Your medicine already turns down your appetite. Fasting on top of it can push you from eating less to eating too little. Aim for a calorie deficit, not a nutrition deficit.

Start gentle

Most people on a GLP-1 do great with a 12:12 window. Closing the kitchen after dinner is often all you need.

Protein comes first

Experts suggest about 80 to 120 g of protein a day while you're losing weight, split into 20 to 40 g per meal.6 If your window makes that hard, widen it.

Lift something

Strength training plus protein is the best way to keep your muscle while the scale drops.6 Try for at least two days a week.8

Sip all day

Fluids help with nausea and constipation, two common GLP-1 side effects. I hydrate today for tomorrow.

Take insulin or a sulfonylurea (like glipizide or glyburide)?

Talk with your prescriber before you fast. These medicines plus fasting can drop your blood sugar too low. Your medicine plan may need to change first.7

Step 3

Fasting claims, checked

You'll hear a lot of big promises about fasting. Here's what the research supports today.

Supported
"Fasting lowers insulin, so your body uses stored fat."
True. After several hours without food, insulin drops and your body shifts to burning stored fuel, including fat.3
Not proven
"Fasting burns more fat than other ways of cutting calories."
In a year-long trial, fasting plus a calorie goal worked about the same as the calorie goal alone. Body fat, belly fat, and lab results matched too.1
Partly true
"Fasting makes it easier to eat less."
For many people, yes. Closing the kitchen ends late-night snacking. In one 12-week study with no calorie goals, though, people ate about the same and lost little weight.2
Not proven
"After 16 hours, autophagy (your cells' cleanup crew) kicks in."
That timeline comes from animal studies. Scientists can't yet measure this well in people, and no one knows the hour it starts in humans.4
Partly true
"Fasting raises growth hormone, which protects your muscle."
Growth hormone does rise during a fast.5 In one trial, though, the fasting group lost more muscle.2 Protein and strength training are what protect muscle.
Partly true
"Fasting improves cholesterol and blood sugar."
These numbers often get better, mostly because of the weight you lose. Other ways of losing the same weight help about as much.1,3
Not proven
"Fasting lowers your cancer risk."
Animal studies look hopeful. There are no strong human studies that show this.3
Not proven
"Women should match their fasting window to their cycle."
There's very little research on this. If one week of the month feels harder, it's fine to shorten your window that week.
Step 4

Break your fast with protein

Your first meal sets up the rest of your day. Lead with protein, add color, and keep sugary carbs small.

Protein (eat it first): eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, fish, chicken, turkey, tofu.

Vegetables and some fruit: greens, peppers, broccoli, berries.

A little healthy fat and fiber: avocado, olive oil, nuts, seeds, sweet potato.

Step 5

What to sip while the kitchen is closed

Go ahead

  • Water, plain or sparklingAdd lemon, ginger, or a pinch of salt.
  • Black coffeeSwitch to decaf if you feel jittery. On a GLP-1, coffee can upset a queasy stomach.
  • Plain or herbal teaCinnamon, ginger, or turmeric are fine.

Small amounts

  • A splash of cream, or 1 tsp MCT or coconut oilEach adds about 40 calories. Fine for weight loss. MCT oil doesn't boost metabolism in any way you'd notice.
  • Collagen (1 scoop, about 35 calories)It's missing some building blocks, so don't count it toward your protein goal.
  • Broth (under 50 calories)Helpful on longer fasts, especially for salt and fluids.
  • Apple cider vinegar, well dilutedCan bother your stomach and teeth. Skip it if you're nauseous.
  • Zero-calorie sweetenersNo calories. If they make you crave sweets, leave them out.

Ends your fast

  • Juice, soda, and sweet tea
  • Sweet coffee drinks and lattes
  • Smoothies and protein shakesSave these for your eating window. A shake is a great way to hit protein when food feels like too much.
  • Alcohol
Step 6

Listen to your body

Shorten your window if you notice:

  • Dizziness or feeling lightheaded
  • Shaky, sweaty, or suddenly very hungry
  • Headaches that don't go away
  • You can't reach your protein goal
  • Worse sleep, mood, or energy
  • Food is all you can think about

Moving your body while fasting? Walks and easy workouts are fine. Save hard lifting for after a meal.

Talk to your doctor before fasting if you:

  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Have had an eating disorder
  • Have type 1 diabetes, or take insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Are under 18, underweight, or frail
  • Take medicines that need food

How this fits your plan

Small steps that carry a lot of weight

Meal timing is one of our eight pillars. It works best alongside the others, especially protein and hydration. Pick one window, keep it for a couple of weeks, and let it become normal before you change anything else.
