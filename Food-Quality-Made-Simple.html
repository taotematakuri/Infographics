Food Quality Made Simple

You don't need a perfect, all-organic kitchen. The biggest upgrade is eating more real food and fewer foods that come from a factory. Labels like organic and grass-fed are a smaller bonus.

Pillar: The Real Food Regimen
+500calories a day people ate on an ultra-processed diet1
1
When you eat less, every bite counts more.

In a carefully controlled study, people ate about 500 more calories a day on ultra-processed food than on whole food, even though both diets offered the same nutrients. They gained weight on one and lost it on the other.1 Aim for a calorie deficit, not a nutrition deficit.

Step 1

Know where your food falls

Most of your cart should come from the left side of this line. The right side is fine sometimes, just not most of the time.

Whole

As it grew

Fresh or frozen fruits and veggies, eggs, meat, fish, beans, nuts.

Lightly processed

Cut, cooked, or canned

Plain Greek yogurt, canned tuna, frozen berries, oats, cheese, olive oil.

Processed

A few added ingredients

Fresh bread, smoked salmon, canned soup. Read the label.

Ultra-processed

Made in a factory

Chips, candy, soda, sugary cereal, most packaged snacks. Long ingredient lists you can't picture in a kitchen.

Step 2

On a GLP-1? Small plate, high quality.

Your appetite is smaller now, so there's less room for empty calories. Fill the room you have with protein, produce, and foods that look like food.

Protein first

Aim for about 80 to 120 g a day while you're losing weight.2 Eggs, canned fish, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, and beans are budget-friendly.

Greasy food can backfire

Fried and heavy, fatty foods often make GLP-1 nausea worse. Grilled, baked, and steamed usually sit better.2

Step 3

Organic: what it means and what it doesn't

The USDA Organic seal means at least 95% of ingredients are organic: grown without most synthetic pesticides or fertilizers, no GMOs, and animals raised without antibiotics or added hormones.3

Supported
"Organic food has less pesticide residue."
Yes, consistently. Organic crops also tend to have less cadmium, a heavy metal.4
Partly true
"Organic food is more nutritious."
Some organic crops have a bit more antioxidants. Vitamin and mineral levels are mostly the same.4,5
Partly true
"Pesticides on food cause cancer, hormone problems, and more."
Those links come mostly from heavy exposure, like farm work. On store produce, almost all samples test below the EPA's safety limits.6 Lower is still reasonable to aim for.

On a budget?

  • Regular produce is far better than skipping produce.
  • Frozen fruits and veggies are picked ripe and cost less.
  • Rinse produce under running water. Soap isn't needed.
  • If you buy some organic, choose the foods you eat most often.

The "Dirty Dozen" list

The Environmental Working Group publishes a yearly list of produce with the most and least pesticide residue. It's an advocacy group, not a government agency, but the list can help you decide where to spend.

Step 4

Additives: read the label, don't panic

Supported
"Red No. 3 is a problem dye."
The FDA revoked its use in food in January 2025. Food makers have until January 2027 to remove it.7 The FDA is also pushing companies to phase out other petroleum-based dyes.
Partly true
"Artificial colors cause hyperactivity."
In one well-known trial, some children became more hyperactive after drinks with artificial colors.8 The effect varies from child to child.
Partly true
"Additives cause weight gain."
The strongest evidence is about ultra-processed food as a whole, not one single additive.1 Choosing whole foods takes care of most additives anyway.
Simple rule: pick packaged foods with short ingredient lists made of things you'd recognize in a kitchen.
Step 5

Meat, egg, and fish labels decoded

Definitions from the USDA unless noted.9

LabelWhat it actually means
NaturalNo artificial ingredients and minimally processed. Says nothing about how the animal was raised.
OrganicOrganic feed, outdoor access, no antibiotics or added hormones.
Grass-fedMostly grass diet. Some may eat grain at the end. Look for a third-party seal to be sure.
Grass-finishedGrass for the animal's whole life.
No hormones added (chicken, pork)Hormones are already banned in poultry and pork, so this is true of all of it.
Cage-freeHens aren't in cages but may still be crowded indoors.
Free-rangeHens have some access to the outdoors.
Pasture-raisedMore outdoor space. No USDA definition, so look for a third-party seal.
Wild-caught vs. farmed fishBoth are healthy choices. Farmed salmon often has as much omega-3 as wild. The FDA says the benefits of eating fish outweigh the risks for most people.10
Partly true
"Grass-fed beef has more omega-3s."
Yes, more than grain-fed.11 Beef is still a small source, though. Salmon and sardines give you far more.
Not proven
"Grass-fed beef's CLA helps you lose weight."
Even high-dose CLA supplements led to less than 2 pounds of loss in studies, and the amount in beef is much smaller.12
Step 6

Upgrade in this order

Start at the top. Move down only when the step above feels easy.

1

Swap one ultra-processed snack for a whole food

Chips become an apple and cheese stick. Cookies become Greek yogurt and berries.

2

Put protein and produce on every plate

Fresh, frozen, or canned all count.

3

Read ingredient lists on packaged foods

Shorter is usually better.

4

Upgrade labels where your budget allows

Organic produce you eat most, pasture-raised eggs, grass-fed beef if you eat a lot of it.

How this fits your plan

Real food, one swap at a time

The Real Food Regimen is about eating food that looks like food. Pick one swap from Step 6 this week and make it a habit before adding the next.
