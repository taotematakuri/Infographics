Hormones and metabolism

Growth hormone: your body's night-shift builder

Growth hormone helps kids grow, and it keeps working long after we stop getting taller. Here is what it does, how your body controls it, what turns it up or down, and an honest look at the shots, pills, and peptides people are using to chase it.

Part one · What is growth hormone?

A messenger with a long job list

Growth hormone (also called GH, HGH, or somatotropin) is a protein hormone made by the pituitary gland. It travels through your blood and tells tissues all over your body to grow, repair, and use fuel in certain ways.

Hormone = a chemical messenger made in one part of the body that travels through the blood to change what happens somewhere else.
Childhood

Helps kids grow taller

Signals the growth plates at the ends of bones to keep lengthening until puberty ends.

All ages

Builds and keeps muscle

Helps your body make protein and hold on to lean tissue.

All ages

Keeps bones strong

Supports bone building and bone density across your lifetime.

Adulthood

Burns stored fat for fuel

Tells fat cells to release stored fat so it can be used for energy. This is called lipolysis.

Adulthood

Shapes blood sugar

Pushes blood sugar up between meals and overnight so your brain stays fueled. Too much of it can work against insulin.

All ages

Repairs and restores

Supports the repair of skin, tendons, and other tissues, much of it overnight.

Part two · How the signal works

A gas pedal, a brake, and a thermostat

Your brain does not release growth hormone in a steady drip. It sends it out in bursts, then checks the result and adjusts. That loop is what keeps levels from running too low or too high.

1

The brain presses the gas

The hypothalamus sends GHRH, a "make growth hormone now" signal. Ghrelin from an empty stomach can also nudge the pituitary to release a burst.

2

The pituitary releases a burst

Growth hormone pours into your blood in pulses, several times a day. It only lasts a few minutes in the blood, so the pulses matter.

3

The liver makes IGF-1

Much of growth hormone's work is handed off to IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1). IGF-1 lasts much longer in the blood, which is why doctors usually test IGF-1 instead of growth hormone itself.

4

The brain hits the brake

When IGF-1 rises, the brain releases somatostatin, which slows things down. This feedback loop is your body's thermostat.

Part three · Your natural levels

What turns it up, and what turns it down

Before anyone reaches for a shot, it helps to know that everyday habits move growth hormone in real, measurable ways. Many of the things that lower it are also the things that make weight loss harder.

Turns it up
  • +
    Deep sleep. The largest pulse of the day comes in the first stretch of deep sleep. A steady bedtime protects it.
  • +
    Hard exercise. Strength training and higher intensity intervals trigger a strong growth hormone release.
  • +
    Time between meals. Levels rise when insulin is low, such as overnight or during a longer gap between meals.
  • +
    Losing belly fat. As deep belly fat shrinks, growth hormone output tends to recover toward normal.
Turns it down
  • −
    Extra belly fat. People with obesity release much less growth hormone. Some of this is caused by the extra weight, not just age.
  • −
    Short or broken sleep. Less deep sleep means a smaller nighttime burst.
  • −
    Late, sugary snacks. A spike of insulin near bedtime can blunt the nighttime release.
  • −
    Alcohol in the evening. Drinking before bed reduces the growth hormone released during sleep.
  • −
    Age. Output slowly declines each decade. This is normal, and it is not the same thing as a medical deficiency.

The good news for weight loss

In studies of people who lost a large amount of weight, their 24-hour growth hormone pattern moved back toward normal. Sleep, strength training, protein, and steady fat loss are the small steps that carry a lot of weight here.

Part four · Why people use it

Real medical needs, and a popular wellness trend

Growth hormone treatment changes lives for people who truly lack it. The wellness version is a different story, with far less evidence behind it.

Approved medical uses
  • Children with growth hormone deficiency or certain genetic and kidney conditions that limit growth
  • Adults with a true, tested deficiency, often after pituitary surgery, tumors, radiation, or head injury
  • Muscle wasting from HIV, and short bowel syndrome
Off-label wellness uses
  • "Anti-aging" and more energy
  • Building muscle or improving athletic performance
  • Losing fat, especially belly fat
  • Faster recovery, better sleep, or healthier skin
Off-label = using a drug for something it was not approved to treat. Doctors do this legally with many drugs. Growth hormone is an exception: in the U.S., giving or selling it for anti-aging or athletic use is against federal law. All of the products on this page are also banned in competitive sports.
Part five · The medications and peptides

Two ways to raise growth hormone

Every product people talk about falls into one of two strategies: add growth hormone directly, or press a button that tells your own pituitary to release more.

Strategy one · Add it directly

Synthetic growth hormone

Somatropin is lab-made human growth hormone given as a shot. It is the strongest option and works whether or not the pituitary is healthy. Newer once-weekly versions now exist for diagnosed deficiency.

Strategy two · Press a button

Secretagogues and peptides

These are small proteins (peptides) or pills that signal your pituitary to release your own growth hormone. They only work if the pituitary still has growth hormone to give, and the body's brakes still apply.

Part six · Growth hormone and weight loss

It moves the needle, but only a little

Growth hormone does tell fat cells to let go of stored fat. The question is how much that matters in real people. Here is what the research shows.

~2 kg

Small shift in body makeup

In healthy older adults given growth hormone, fat mass dropped by about two kilograms (about four pounds) and lean mass rose by about the same. Body weight barely changed.

Liu et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2007
No gain

Bigger muscle is not stronger muscle

Those same studies found no meaningful improvement in strength or fitness. Much of the "lean mass" gain can be extra fluid.

Liu et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2007
~15%

Belly fat, in one condition

Tesamorelin reduced deep belly fat by about 15 percent over six months in people with HIV-related fat buildup. It is not approved for general weight loss.

Falutz et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2007
Keeping it honest: Growth hormone can raise blood sugar and make your body less responsive to insulin, which works against metabolic health. For comparison, GLP-1 medications like semaglutide led to an average weight loss of about 15 percent of body weight in major trials. Growth hormone and its peptides are not weight-loss drugs and are not a replacement for proven treatments.
Part seven · If you take a GLP-1 medication

Protect your muscle the proven way

Many people on a GLP-1 hear about growth hormone peptides as a way to hold on to muscle. The tools with the best evidence are simpler, safer, and already in your hands.

  • Lift something. Strength training two or three times a week is the most reliable way to keep muscle while you lose weight.
  • Hit your protein. Protein at every meal gives your muscles the building blocks they need. A calorie deficit should never become a nutrition deficit.
  • Guard your sleep. Deep sleep is when your own growth hormone does its biggest work.
  • Watch for crossed wires. MK-677 and GHRP-6 can increase hunger and raise blood sugar, which pulls against what your GLP-1 is doing.
  • Tell your prescriber. If you are using or thinking about any peptide, including one bought online, bring it up. Your prescriber can watch your blood sugar and labs.
  • Remember the big picture. Your GLP-1 is a tool, not a finish line. The habits you build now are what keep the results.
Part eight · Safety and the legal side

More is not automatically better

When growth hormone or IGF-1 runs higher than your body needs, side effects are common. In studies of healthy older adults, these were the problems people had most often.

Swelling

Fluid buildup in the hands, feet, and legs.

Joint and muscle pain

Aches and stiffness, especially early on.

Carpal tunnel

Numbness and tingling in the hands.

Higher blood sugar

Less insulin sensitivity and a higher risk of type 2 diabetes.

Breast tissue growth in men

Called gynecomastia.

Unknowns over time

IGF-1 helps cells grow, so long-term cancer risk is a real question. Growth hormone is not used in people with active cancer.

The legal and quality picture

Real growth hormone is prescription only, and distributing it for anti-aging, bodybuilding, or athletic performance is a federal crime in the U.S. Most growth hormone peptides are not FDA-approved. Several are on the FDA's list of substances that raise significant safety concerns when compounded. Products sold online as "research chemicals" or "not for human use" have no guarantee of purity, dose, or sterility, and testing has repeatedly found products that do not match their labels.

Part nine · Talking with your doctor

Good questions to bring to the visit

Whether growth hormone or a peptide makes sense for you is a decision for you and a licensed clinician who can test, weigh the risks, and monitor you over time.

1

Has my level been tested?

An IGF-1 blood test is a starting point. A true deficiency diagnosis usually needs a stimulation test.

2

What should come first?

Ask about sleep, strength training, protein, and fat loss before adding any medication.

3

Where does it come from?

Is it an FDA-approved product, a compounded product, or something bought online?

4

How will we monitor it?

Plan for follow-up labs, including blood sugar and IGF-1, and a check for side effects.

5

Does it mix with my other medicines?

Especially a GLP-1, insulin, thyroid medicine, or estrogen, which can all change how growth hormone works.

6

What is the goal, and when do we stop?

Agree on what success looks like and how you will know if it is not working.
