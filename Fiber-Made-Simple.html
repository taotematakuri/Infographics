Fiber Made Simple

Fiber is the part of plant food your body can't digest. It passes through you mostly whole, and it does a lot of good on the way: it keeps you full, regular, and steadier after meals.

Add it slowly, and drink more water as you go.

Your gut bacteria need time to adjust. Raise your fiber by about 2 to 3 grams a day. Fiber soaks up water, so sip more as you add it, or it can make constipation worse instead of better.

Step 1

How much do you need?

Daily goals from the National Academies.2 Most adults get about half of this.

Step 2

Two kinds, two jobs

Soluble fiber

Turns into a gel

Slows how fast sugar enters your blood and helps lower LDL cholesterol.

Find it in: oats, beans, lentils, apples, citrus, chia seeds.

Insoluble fiber

Adds bulk

Helps food move through your gut and keeps you regular.

Find it in: whole grains, nuts, seeds, vegetable and fruit skins.

Both

Feeds your gut bacteria

Many fibers are food for your good gut bacteria. Most plants have some of each kind, so variety covers you.

Step 3

On a GLP-1? Fiber needs a gentle hand.

GLP-1 medicines slow how fast your stomach empties. That helps you feel full, but it also makes constipation one of the most common side effects. Fiber and fluids help, as long as you go slowly.3

Build up a little at a time

A huge salad on a slow stomach can mean bloating and nausea. Add one fiber food at a time.

Pair fiber with fluids

Sip through the day, not all at once. I hydrate today for tomorrow.

Cooked beats raw when you're queasy

Soft cooked veggies, oatmeal, soaked chia, and canned pumpkin are easier on a slow stomach than raw greens.

Protein still comes first

Small stomach, small plate. Eat your protein first, then your fiber foods. Beans and lentils give you both.

Call your provider if…

You haven't had a bowel movement in several days, or you have bad belly pain, swelling, or vomiting. A fiber supplement like psyllium can help some people, but ask first if you take other medicines, since it can change how they absorb.

Step 5

What a 30-gram day looks like

Breakfast · about 10 g

1 cup cooked oatmeal, ½ cup raspberries, 1 tbsp ground flax. Stir in Greek yogurt or protein powder for protein.

Snack · about 3.5 g

1 oz almonds (about 23).

Lunch · about 10 g

Lentil soup made with ½ cup lentils plus carrots and celery.

Dinner · about 9 g

Salmon (0 g fiber, lots of protein), a medium baked sweet potato with skin, 1 cup roasted broccoli.

Total: about 32 grams. Spread it across the day so your gut doesn't get it all at once.

Step 6

Fiber claims, checked

Supported
"Fiber lowers your risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, and colon cancer."
A large review found people eating 25 to 29 grams a day had lower risk of all three, compared with people eating little fiber.5
Supported
"Fiber helps with weight loss."
In one diet study, eating more fiber was one of the strongest predictors of who lost weight, no matter which diet they followed.6
Partly true
"Fiber prevents blood sugar spikes."
It slows the rise, especially soluble fiber. A big bowl of sugar is still a big bowl of sugar, even with fiber on the side.
Partly true
"Fiber lowers blood pressure and balances cholesterol."
Soluble fiber lowers LDL cholesterol a bit, and blood pressure drops a little in studies. The effects are real but modest.5
Step 7

Easy ways to fit in more

Eat the peel

Apples, pears, potatoes, and carrots keep much of their fiber in the skin.

Lean on beans

Beans and lentils bring fiber and protein together. Toss them into soups, salads, and chili.

Swap your grains

Brown rice, quinoa, barley, or oats in place of white rice and white bread.

Snack on plants

Fruit, raw veggies with hummus, nuts, or air-popped popcorn instead of chips.

How this fits your plan

Small steps that carry a lot of weight

Fiber is part of eating real food, and it makes cutting back on sugar easier because you stay full longer. Add one high-fiber food this week. Next week, add another.
