The Five Rs of Gut Health

Your gut is home to trillions of bacteria that help digest food, make some vitamins, and train your immune system. You can help them with everyday habits more than with special products.

The habits with the best proof are the simple ones.

Fiber, fermented foods, movement, sleep, and stress care all have good research behind them. Many "gut healing" supplements and tests have much less. This guide shows which is which.

Step 1

Gut facts, checked

Partly true
"80% of your immune system lives in your gut."
A large share of your immune cells, roughly 70%, sit in and around your gut lining.2
Partly true
"Your gut makes 95% of your feel-good serotonin."
Your gut makes about 90% of your body's serotonin, but that serotonin works in your gut and blood. It doesn't travel to your brain, which makes its own.3
Supported
"Around 70 million Americans have digestive problems."
Federal estimates put it at 60 to 70 million people. You're far from alone.4
Step 2

On a GLP-1? Your gut is adjusting too.

Nausea, constipation, diarrhea, reflux, and bloating are common, especially after a dose increase. Most of it gets better as your body adjusts. A few habits help a lot.5

Smaller meals, slower bites

Stop at the first sign of full. Your stomach empties more slowly now.

Steady fluids

Sip through the day. It helps with constipation and keeps you from getting dehydrated if you have diarrhea.

Go easy on greasy

Fried and very fatty meals are the most common nausea triggers.

Skip big experiments for now

Starting a strict elimination diet while starting a GLP-1 makes it hard to tell what's causing what.

Call your provider right away for

Severe belly pain that won't let up (especially if it spreads to your back), vomiting so you can't keep fluids down, black or bloody stools, or yellowing skin or eyes.

Step 3

The five Rs, with what the research says

The five Rs come from functional medicine. Some steps are well supported. Others rely on supplements with little proof. Each step shows its verdict.

1

Remove what bothers your gut

Partly supported
  • Good bets: cut back on alcohol and ultra-processed food. Ask your provider about NSAIDs like ibuprofen, which can irritate the gut lining.
  • Elimination diets can help with specific problems. The low-FODMAP diet has good evidence for IBS.6 Keep them short (a few weeks) and guided, then add foods back one at a time.
  • Skip at-home IgG "food sensitivity" tests. Allergy experts say they don't diagnose sensitivities.7
  • Yeast or bacterial overgrowth (like SIBO) needs real testing and treatment from a doctor, not guesswork with herbs.
2

Replace digestive helpers

Mostly not proven
  • Lactase enzyme (for lactose intolerance) works. General digestive enzyme blends haven't been shown to help most people.
  • Apple cider vinegar and HCl (stomach acid) pills have no good evidence for raising stomach acid. Most reflux isn't caused by low acid, and acid pills can hurt if you have an ulcer. Don't take them without your provider.
  • Bile-support herbs like milk thistle and dandelion haven't been shown to improve digestion.
3

Reinoculate with good bacteria

Supported (food)
  • Fermented foods like yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, and kimchi raised gut bacteria variety and lowered inflammation markers in a Stanford study.8 Try one or two servings a day.
  • Prebiotic foods feed your good bacteria: oats, onions, garlic, leeks, asparagus, bananas, apples, flaxseed, beans.
  • Probiotic pills help in a few specific cases, like diarrhea from antibiotics. For general gut health, the evidence is weak, and gut doctors don't recommend them for most conditions.9
4

Repair the gut lining

Not proven
  • "Leaky gut" is real in some diseases, like celiac and Crohn's. As a cause of general symptoms, it isn't an established diagnosis, and home tests can't measure it well.10
  • Bone broth, collagen, L-glutamine, aloe, slippery elm, marshmallow root, and DGL haven't been shown in good studies to repair the gut. Aloe latex is a strong laxative and can be harmful.11
  • Best bet: a varied diet with enough protein, fiber, and fish or other omega-3 foods.
5

Rebalance your lifestyle

Supported
  • Move at least 150 minutes a week.12 Exercise is linked to a more varied gut microbiome.
  • Sleep on a regular schedule and dim screens before bed.
  • Manage stress. Your gut and brain talk both ways. Walks, prayer or meditation, breathing, music, and time outside all count.
  • Eat plants in variety: fruits, veggies, whole grains, beans, nuts.
Step 4

See a doctor, not a supplement aisle, if you have

  • Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stools
  • Trouble swallowing or vomiting that keeps coming back
  • Weight loss you can't explain, or faster than your plan expects
  • Diarrhea that wakes you up at night
  • Belly symptoms and you're 45 or older and haven't had colon cancer screening
How this fits your plan

Feed the good guys

The two habits that do the most for your gut are already in our pillars: real food with plenty of fiber, and steady hydration. Add one fermented food this week.
