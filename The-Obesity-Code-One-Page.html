The Obesity Code

The Obesity Code, in one page

Obesity is a hormonal problem, not a willpower problem. Chronically high insulin raises the body's fat "set point," so the fat gain drives the overeating, not the other way around. Lower insulin, and weight follows.

Fung's five rules: what to eat
Rule 1

Cut added sugars

The biggest insulin driver. Cut it in every form.

Rule 2

Cut refined grains

Less bread, pasta, and refined cereal.

Rule 3

Moderate protein

Enough, not excessive.

Rule 4

Eat natural fats

Olive oil, nuts, avocado, whole foods.

Rule 5

Add protective factors

Fiber and vinegar to blunt spikes.

The other half: when to eat

Give insulin time to fall

Stop grazing. Constant snacking keeps insulin up all day. Use an eating window or regular fasting so your body gets long stretches to switch from storing fat to burning it.

The one idea to remember

Your body has a fat thermostat

Cut calories alone and the body defends its set point with more hunger and a slower metabolism. Lowering insulin over time is what resets the thermostat down, so the loss actually sticks.

Why plateaus happen: your metabolism adapts to defend your weight. That is normal, not failure. Stay the course.
Six quick wins
Drop the sugary drinks

Soda and juice are the fastest insulin hit.

Swap refined for whole

Whole foods over anything white and processed.

Don't fear natural fat

It keeps you full without the insulin spike.

Add fiber and vinegar

They soften the glucose response of a meal.

Stop snacking

Let meals end; give insulin a real break.

Sleep and de-stress

Both lower the cortisol that raises insulin.

Your one line: eat real food, cut the sugar and refined carbs, give your body real breaks from eating, and be patient with a metabolism that is built to adapt.

Educational use only. This is general lifestyle education, not medical advice. Any big change, especially fasting while on a GLP-1 or other medication, is a conversation for you and your own prescribing clinician.
