Absorb More From Your Food

A food label tells you what's in a food. It doesn't tell you how much your body actually takes in. That amount is called bioavailability, and a few easy food pairings and cooking choices can raise it.

Pairings help a little. Variety helps a lot.

Smart pairings are a nice bonus, not a rule to stress over. Eating a wide mix of whole foods across the day covers most of your needs on its own.

Step 1

On a GLP-1? Smaller meals make absorption matter more.

When you eat less food, you take in fewer vitamins and minerals too. Experts flag iron, calcium, vitamin D, and B12 as ones to watch during GLP-1 treatment.2

Make each bite count

Protein first, then colorful produce. Use the pairings below to get more from what you eat.

Ask about labs and a multivitamin

If your appetite is very low for weeks, ask your provider whether you need blood work or a daily multivitamin.

Step 2

Food pairings that help

Each pairing shows how strong the evidence is.

Supported
Vitamin C+Plant iron

Iron from plants (beans, lentils, spinach, tofu) is harder to absorb. Vitamin C in the same meal helps a lot.3

Try: lemon in lentil soup, peppers with black beans, strawberries on a spinach salad.

Supported
Vitamin D+Calcium

Without enough vitamin D, you absorb only 10 to 15% of the calcium you eat.1 They don't need to be in the same meal, just the same day.

Try: salmon with broccoli, eggs with a glass of milk.

Supported
Vitamins A, D, E, K+Healthy fat

These vitamins need fat to be absorbed. Adding avocado to a salad raised carotenoid absorption several times over in one study.4

Try: kale sautéed in olive oil, sweet potato with avocado.

Supported
Tomatoes+Olive oil + heat

Cooking tomatoes with a little fat makes their lycopene easier to absorb.4

Try: tomato sauce simmered with olive oil.

Supported
Plant zinc+Animal protein

Beans and grains have phytates that block zinc. Eating them with meat, fish, or eggs, or soaking and sprouting them, helps.5

Try: chili with beef and kidney beans.

Partly true
Vitamin D+Magnesium

Your body needs magnesium to activate vitamin D.6 The reverse effect is less clear. Leafy greens, nuts, and seeds cover magnesium.

Try: eggs with sautéed spinach.

Partly true
Calcium+Inulin fiber

Inulin raised calcium absorption in teens taking it as a supplement.7 Onions, garlic, leeks, and asparagus have smaller amounts.

Partly true
Turmeric+Black pepper

The "2,000% better" number came from a large dose of pepper extract with 2 grams of curcumin.8 A pinch of pepper in cooking does much less. Turmeric pills, especially with pepper extract, have been linked to rare liver injury.9

Partly true
Green tea+Lemon

Lemon helped tea antioxidants survive digestion in a lab test-tube study.10 It hasn't been tested in people, but it's a harmless habit.

Step 3

Pairings that work against you

Coffee or tea with an iron-rich meal

Both lower iron absorption. Drink them an hour or more before or after.3

Calcium pills with iron-rich food

Calcium competes with iron. If you take a calcium supplement, take it at a different meal.3

Unsoaked beans and grains

Soaking, sprouting, or fermenting lowers the phytates that block iron and zinc.5

Step 4

Cook to keep your vitamins

Water-soluble vitamins like vitamin C and the B vitamins leak into cooking water. Shorter cooking with less water keeps more of them.11

A rough ranking for water-soluble vitamins, from keeps the most to loses the most. Bars show rank, not exact percentages, and results vary by vegetable and cooking time. Heat also has an upside: cooking makes lycopene and beta-carotene easier to absorb.

How this fits your plan

Get more from every plate

Real food and enough protein do most of the work. Pairings are the finishing touch. Try one new pairing at dinner this week.
