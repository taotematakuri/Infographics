What is "glycemic load"?

Glycemic load, or GL, is a score that tells you how much a food will raise your blood sugar. It mixes together two things: how fast a food turns into sugar, and how much sugar is actually in your serving.

Think of it like rain. Glycemic index (GI) is how hard the rain is coming down. Glycemic load (GL) is how wet you actually get, because it also counts how long you stand in it. A hard rain for one second barely touches you. A light rain for an hour soaks you through. Your blood sugar works the same way.

Glycemic Load  =  GI × grams of carbs in your serving100
Low GL is 10 or under • Medium is 11 to 19 • High is 20 or more (per serving)

This matters because GI alone can trick you. Watermelon has a scary-high GI of 76, about the same as a doughnut. But one slice of watermelon has very little sugar in it, so its glycemic load is only about 8. The doughnut lands around 17. The load, not just the speed, is what your body feels.

Inside your body

What a high glycemic load actually does

When you eat a sweetener with a high glycemic load, here is the chain of events that follows.

STEP 1

You eat it

Your gut breaks the sugar down and sends glucose into your blood.

STEP 2

Blood sugar spikes

The higher the load, the bigger and faster the spike goes up.

STEP 3

Insulin rushes in

Your pancreas floods out insulin to pull the sugar out of your blood.

STEP 4

The crash

Blood sugar drops fast, leaving you tired, hungry, and craving more.

Do this many times a day, for years, and your body has to keep pumping out insulin. Over time cells can stop listening to it well, which is called insulin resistance. That is a big step on the road to weight gain, type 2 diabetes, and heart trouble. Choosing lower-load sweeteners keeps those spikes small and your energy steady.

Read the fine print

Four things the numbers do not tell you

A low glycemic load is a good sign, but it is not the whole story. Keep these in mind before you pick a favorite.

The zero-load winners

Monk fruit & stevia

These pass through your body without raising blood sugar, so their load is zero. They are also very strong, often 200 times sweeter than sugar, so you only need a pinch. Great for keeping spikes flat.

Low score, hidden cost

Agave's fructose trap

Agave scores low because it is mostly fructose, which barely moves blood sugar. But your liver has to process all that fructose, and too much is linked to fatty liver and higher triglycerides. Low load does not always mean healthy.

Real food bonus

Date powder

Date powder is just ground whole dates, so it brings fiber, potassium, and antioxidants along with the sugar. The fiber slows things down. It is still sugar, so use it wisely, but it beats the refined stuff spoon for spoon.

The one to limit

Corn syrup & HFCS

This is the heavy hitter: fast, cheap, and hiding in sodas, candy, sauces, and packaged snacks. It drives the biggest spikes and is the easiest to overeat because it is in so many foods at once.

Bottom line

Four things to remember

  • Load beats index

    Glycemic load counts both speed and amount, so it is the number that best matches what your body feels.

  • Smaller spikes, steadier you

    Lower-load choices keep insulin calm, which supports steady energy, fewer cravings, and healthier weight over time.

  • Zero-load does not mean anything-goes

    Monk fruit and stevia are great tools, but very sweet tastes still shape your cravings. Dial down the sweet overall.

  • Watch the amount, not just the type

    A big pour of a "healthy" syrup can still raise the load. Portion size is always part of the math.
