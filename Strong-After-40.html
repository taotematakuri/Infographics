Strong After 40

Fitness and nutrition for women in their 40s and beyond. Your body changes as you approach and pass menopause. Those changes are normal, and much of what follows can be slowed with strength training and protein.

You can still get stronger at any age.

In one trial, women past menopause with low bone density did supervised heavy lifting twice a week for eight months. Their bones got denser, and their strength and balance improved.2

Step 1

What changes around menopause

Perimenopause, the years before your last period, can last from a few months to several years. Estrogen rises and falls unevenly, then drops. Some women have hot flashes, poor sleep, and weight gain. Some have few symptoms.

Bone

Bone loss speeds up

Lower estrogen means bone breaks down faster than it's rebuilt, especially in the first years after menopause.

Muscle

Muscle slowly shrinks

Adults lose roughly 3 to 8% of muscle per decade after 30 without strength training.3

Blood sugar

Insulin works less well

Your body needs more insulin to handle the same carbs, which makes weight gain around the middle easier.

Heart

LDL cholesterol rises

LDL goes up around the menopause transition, separate from aging alone.4

Step 2

On a GLP-1? Protect your muscle and bones.

When you lose weight, some of it is muscle, and on GLP-1s that can be a quarter or more of what you lose.5 After menopause, you're already losing muscle and bone, so this matters even more.

Lift every week

Strength training is the strongest signal to your body to keep muscle and bone while fat comes off.

Hit your protein

About 80 to 120 g a day while you're losing weight, spread over meals.5 Protein snacks count, and they help when meals are small.

Calcium and vitamin D

Women 51 and older need 1,200 mg of calcium a day (1,000 mg before 51) and 600 IU of vitamin D.6 Food first: dairy, fortified milks, canned salmon with bones, greens.

Ask about a bone scan

Screening is advised for women 65 and older, and earlier after menopause if you have risk factors.7 Big weight loss is worth mentioning to your provider.

Step 3

Make your workouts count

Lift a weight you can move 8 to 12 times with good form before you need to rest. Do 2 to 4 sets.8 Learn the form first, then add weight slowly.

Upper body

Band row

Anchor a band. Pull your elbows back and squeeze your shoulder blades. Keep your neck relaxed.

Upper body

Band pull-apart

Hold a band with straight arms in front of you. Pull your hands apart out to the sides, then return.

Upper body

Skull crusher

Lie on your back, weights straight up. Bend at the elbows to lower the weights toward your shoulders, then press back up.

Upper body

Arnold press

Start with weights at shoulder height, palms facing you. Press up overhead as you turn your palms forward.

Upper body

Quadruped T-raise

On hands and knees with a flat back, lift one arm straight out to the side with a light weight. Switch sides.

Lower body

Goblet squat

Hold a weight at your chest, feet a bit wider than hips. Push your hips back and bend your knees, then stand.

Lower body

Lateral step-up

Stand beside a step. Put the near foot on it and step up, then lower slowly.

Lower body

Monster walk

Band around your ankles, knees slightly bent. Take 10 to 15 steps to the side, then back.

Lower body

Walking lunge

Step forward and lower your back knee toward the floor. Stand and step through with the other leg. Hold weights if you're ready.

Lower body

Side lunge

Step wide to one side and sit back into that hip. Push back to standing.

Step 4

Eat for strength

1

Put protein at every meal

Experts suggest older adults get 1.0 to 1.2 g of protein per kilogram of body weight a day, and more if you're active or ill.9 For a 140-pound woman, that's about 64 to 77 g, and more during GLP-1 weight loss. Aim for 25 to 30 g per meal.

2

Choose nutrient-dense foods

Eat a rainbow of produce. Leafy greens give calcium, vitamin K, and magnesium. Orange veggies give beta-carotene. Berries give vitamin C and fiber. Soy foods may ease hot flashes a little.10

3

Get your omega-3s

Two servings of fatty fish a week, like salmon, sardines, or mackerel.11 Farmed and wild salmon are both good choices. Plant sources include walnuts, chia, flax, and hemp seeds.

4

Build a balanced plate

Protein, fiber-rich veggies, a smart carb like beans or quinoa, and a little healthy fat. Choose foods that look like they came from a farm, not a factory. If you snack, make it protein: Greek yogurt with berries, cottage cheese, nuts, or veggies with hummus.

Step 5

Claims, checked

Supported
"Strength training is the best exercise for women over 40."
It does the most for muscle, bone, and blood sugar. Walking, biking, and swimming still add heart benefits, so do both.1
Partly true
"Bone and muscle loss can be prevented or reversed."
They can be slowed, and sometimes partly reversed, with lifting and good nutrition.2 Some women also need medicine. Ask your provider.
Not true
"Omega-3s lower LDL cholesterol."
Omega-3s lower triglycerides, another blood fat. They don't lower LDL and can raise it slightly.11 Fiber, less saturated fat, and weight loss help LDL.
Partly true
"Skip snacks so your blood sugar can reset."
What and how much you eat matters more than how often. On a GLP-1, a protein snack is often the best way to reach your protein goal.
Not proven
"Processed food bothers you more because detox slows with age."
There's no good evidence for this. Cutting back on ultra-processed food is still a good idea at any age.
Supplements: if you take fish oil or cod liver oil, choose a brand with a USP or NSF seal. Cod liver oil is high in vitamin A, so check with your provider before combining it with a multivitamin.
How this fits your plan

Stronger every year

Protein and movement are two of our eight pillars, and after 40 they work best as a pair. This week, add one strength session and one protein-rich breakfast.
