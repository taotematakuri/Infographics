Smart Food Swaps

Food allergies, intolerances, diabetes, or just trying to eat better? Simple swaps let you keep the meals you love. This guide also shows which "healthy" swaps are better and which only sound better.

Pillar: Power Up with ProteinCut Back on Sugar

⇄
A swap is only better if it fits your goal.

Honey is still sugar. Coconut oil is still fat. Ask what the swap is for: avoiding an allergen, adding protein, or cutting sugar or calories. Then pick the swap that does that job.

Step 1

On a GLP-1? Swap for protein.

With a smaller appetite, the best swaps add protein without adding volume. Experts suggest about 80 to 120 g of protein a day while you're losing weight.2

Sour cream or mayo → plain Greek yogurt

On tacos, potatoes, dips, chicken salad. About 9 more grams of protein per half cup.1

Regular pasta → chickpea or lentil pasta

About double the protein and two to three times the fiber.

Milk → ultra-filtered milk

About 50% more protein, less sugar, and lactose-free.

Cream sauces → blended cottage cheese

Blend until smooth for creamy pasta sauce, dips, or "ice cream" with frozen fruit.

Swaps for cooking

Butter
→
Olive oilHeart-healthy fats. Use 1:1 for sautéing. Same or slightly more calories than butter, so measure it.
Avocado oilMild flavor and good for high heat. Same calories as other oils.
GheeButter with the milk solids removed, so almost no lactose. Still mostly saturated fat.
Soy sauce
→
Coconut aminosFree of gluten and soy, with much less sodium. Slightly sweeter.
TamariOften gluten-free (check the label). About as salty as soy sauce.
Fish sauceVery salty and strong. Use a little and taste as you go.
Cornstarch
→
Arrowroot or tapioca starchBoth thicken sauces. You'll usually need a bit more than cornstarch, so follow the package. Add arrowroot near the end of cooking.
Heavy cream
→
Full-fat canned coconut milkDairy-free, 1:1. Similar saturated fat to cream.
Evaporated skim milk or blended cottage cheeseLighter and higher in protein. Best in sauces and soups.
Yogurt
→
Plain Greek yogurtAbout twice the protein of regular yogurt.
Soy yogurtThe plant yogurt closest to dairy in protein.
Almond or coconut yogurtDairy-free, but much less protein, and calcium varies. Check for added sugar.
Wheat pasta
→
Chickpea or lentil pastaMore protein and fiber, somewhat fewer carbs.
Zucchini noodles or spaghetti squashFar fewer calories and carbs. Add a protein on top.
Peanut butter
→
Sunflower seed butterNut-free. More vitamin E and magnesium.
Almond butterMore vitamin E, calcium, and fiber. Slightly less protein.
Tahini (sesame)Nut-free, but sesame is a major allergen too.
Powdered peanut butterNo allergy? This cuts calories by about two-thirds and keeps most of the protein.
Step 3

Swaps for baking

Wheat flour
→
Gluten-free all-purpose flourUse 1:1. Look for "cup-for-cup" on the bag.
Oat flourUse about 1⅓ to 1½ cups for each cup of wheat flour. Choose certified gluten-free oats if needed.
Almond flourHigher in protein and fat. Often 1:1, but baked goods turn out denser.
Butter
→
Vegan butter or coconut oilUse 1:1 for dairy-free baking.
Unsweetened applesauceReplace up to half the butter to cut calories and keep a good texture.
Pumpkin puréeUse ¾ cup for each cup of butter.
One egg
→
Flax egg1 tbsp ground flax + 2½ tbsp water. Let sit 5 minutes.
Chia egg1 tbsp chia seeds + 3 tbsp water. Let sit 5 minutes.
Mashed banana or applesauce¼ cup per egg. With applesauce, add ½ tsp baking powder. Best in recipes with one or two eggs.
Milk
→
Soy milkThe plant milk closest to dairy in protein. Use 1:1.
Unsweetened almond milkFewest calories, but very little protein.
Oat milkCreamy, but more carbs and calories than other plant milks.
1 cup buttermilk
→
Homemade "buttermilk"1 cup milk or unsweetened plant milk + 1 tbsp lemon juice or vinegar. Let sit 5 minutes.
1 cup sugar
→
Monk fruit or steviaZero calories and don't raise blood sugar. Store blends vary a lot in sweetness, so follow the package.3
HoneyUse ½ to ⅔ cup. Cut other liquid by about ¼ cup and lower the oven 25°F.
Maple syrupUse ¾ cup and cut other liquid by about 3 tbsp.
Coconut sugarUse 1:1. Same calories as sugar.
Step 4

Swap claims, checked

Not true
"Coconut oil boosts your metabolism."
Coconut oil is mostly saturated fat, and it raised LDL cholesterol compared with plant oils in a large review.4 Use it for flavor, not for health.
Not true
"Avocado oil has fewer calories than butter."
A tablespoon of any oil has about 120 calories. Butter has about 100, because part of butter is water.1 Swap oil for its healthier fats, not for calories.
Not true
"Almond yogurt is more nutritious than dairy yogurt."
Most almond yogurts have far less protein, and their calcium varies. Choose them for a dairy allergy, not for nutrition.1
Partly true
"Honey, maple syrup, and coconut sugar are healthier than sugar."
They have tiny amounts of minerals, but your body treats them all as added sugar. Health guidelines count them the same.5
Partly true
"Monk fruit is safe for diabetes."
Pure monk fruit doesn't raise blood sugar. Many blends add erythritol, which a 2023 study linked to blood clotting and heart events.6 That link isn't proven to cause harm, but check the label.
